Plan Of Action · Rooftech Care Ltd · Glenrothes, Fife

Stop Renting Your Enquiries. Start Owning Them.

Prepared for Christopher Gray · 2 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
The One Sentence Version
Where your work comes from, and why that is the problem

You told us your enquiries come from Checkatrade. That is a perfectly good source and plenty of excellent roofers use it. But look at what it actually is:

  • You compete on price, listed beside every other roofer in Fife.
  • The lead is not exclusive. The same homeowner is talking to two or three of your competitors.
  • You do not own the relationship or the profile. Their rules, their fees, their decision.
  • You cannot increase it. If you want more work next month there is no lever to pull.

Meanwhile you own a 19-page website that produces nothing measurable, because it names no town and has no analytics on it. That is the whole plan: turn the thing you already own into a second source of work, so Checkatrade becomes one of your channels rather than the only one.

Week One — Measure, And Put Your Proof Where People See It
#WhatWhy
1Put GA4 into the tag manager you already haveContainer GTM-548KL6C6 loads on every page with nothing behind it. One afternoon. Until it exists, every conversation about what works is guesswork.
2Track form submissions and phone tapsMost of your enquiries are phone calls. None of them are currently attributed to anything.
3Put your Checkatrade rating and reviews on your own homepage⭐ Free, and the single biggest quick win here. Checkatrade is where your work comes from and your own site mentions it once, with no rating, no review count and no review text anywhere.
4Add a short form to all fifteen service pagesYour Gravity Form works, and it is on one page out of nineteen. Somebody reading your flat roofs page at 9pm has nowhere to put their enquiry.
5Claim and complete the Google Business ProfileFree. For a local trade it is often worth more than the website, and it is where "roofer near me" actually resolves.
Three of those five cost nothing but time

And you should do them whether you work with us or not. We would rather tell you that than pretend otherwise.

Weeks Two And Three — The Towns
This is the actual growth, and it is the thing nobody has done

You have nineteen pages. Every one of them is a service — flat roofs, slate, tiled, chimney, leadwork, guttering, fascias, emergency, roof cleaning and the rest. Not one of them is a place.

“Fife” appears eleven times on your homepage. Glenrothes, Kirkcaldy, Dunfermline, Leven, Cupar, St Andrews, Cowdenbeath, Lochgelly, Methil, Burntisland, Markinch, Buckhaven, Dalgety Bay, Inverkeithing and Anstruther appear zero times between them.

That matters because of how people search. Almost nobody types “roofing services”. They type “roofer Kirkcaldy”, “roof repair Dunfermline”, “emergency roofer near me”. A page called “Roof Repairs Fife” competes for one of those. A page called “Roof Repairs in Kirkcaldy” competes for all three.

⭐ You told us you prefer the shorter runs. Town pages are how you bias the work toward them — you rank hardest where you most want to drive, instead of taking whatever the county sends you.

And you already own the expensive half. Nineteen service pages is the slow, costly part of this and it is done. Crossing them against your best six or eight towns is the cheap part.

Week Four — The Review Engine
  • A review request after every single job, automatically, to Google and Checkatrade. Most roofers ask when they remember, which is to say rarely.
  • Real photographs of real roofs, by town, so somebody in Leven can see a job you did in Leven.
  • Your trust signals on the page. Insured appears twice on your site, guarantee once, Checkatrade once. These are the things a homeowner spending £15,000 is actually looking for.
Reviews are the compounding asset in your trade. They raise your Checkatrade position, they feed your Google profile, and they are the difference between two quotes that are otherwise identical.
Only After That — Paid Advertising
⚠️ Not first, and here is why

You are a genuine fit for paid advertising — unlike a lot of trades we look at. Homeowners searching for a roofer have real intent, the job values support the cost, and your 30–40 mile radius is a sensible area to target.

But sending paid clicks into a site with no tracking is paying to learn nothing. You would spend the money and still not know which half worked. That is why it is last, not first.

Once weeks one to four are done, you would be able to see cost per enquiry by town and by service, and increase spend on what works instead of guessing.

The Thing Nobody Has Raised With You
A £100 job and a £20,000 job arrive through the same door

You told us your work runs from around £100 up to £10,000–£20,000 and that you are happy with any size. Fair enough — small jobs keep crews busy and turn into big ones.

But your website makes no distinction anywhere. Roof cleaning and guttering sit in the same list as new roofs, with the same prominence and the same single phone number. So a gutter clear and a full re-roof arrive by the identical route and you cannot tell which is which until you are on the phone, or in the van.

At three or four jobs a week, your constraint is not how many enquiries you get. It is which ones get your Tuesday.

⭐ Two or three qualifying questions on the form, and pages that frame the larger work as the main offer, change the mix without changing the volume. Same number of jobs, bigger average. That is the cheapest growth available to you and it does not need a single extra enquiry.

What We Need From You
  • 1. Your Checkatrade rating, review count and a few real reviews — so we can put your own proof on your own site.
  • 2. Your best six to eight towns, in the order you would most like to work in them.
  • 3. Photographs of finished jobs, with the town if you know it. Phone photos are fine.
  • 4. Logins for the website, the tag manager and the Google Business Profile if it exists.
  • 5. An honest answer on job mix — would you rather have more work, or bigger work? They are different plans.

Nineteen pages, no towns, no tracking, and all your work coming from somebody else's platform.

The expensive part is already built. What is missing is cheap.
