Finding 1 — The Tag Manager Is Installed And Empty
The one that makes every other number unknowable

Google Tag Manager is on the site — container GTM-548KL6C6, loading on every page. That is the hard part, and somebody did it.

There is no Google Analytics behind it. No GA4 property, no measurement ID, nothing. A tag manager with no tags is a delivery van with nothing in the back.

GTM-548KL6C6   present on every page GA4 (G-XXXXXXXXXX)  not found Google Ads tag (AW-)  not found Meta pixel            not found Call tracking          not found

So today nobody can tell you how many people visited, which of your nineteen pages they landed on, which service they were after, or which ones rang you. You are running a roofing business on 3–4 jobs a week with no idea which half of your marketing is working.

Cost to fix: an afternoon. The container is already there. This is the cheapest high-value item on the page and it should be done whoever you work with.

Finding 2 — Nineteen Pages, Not One Town
This is the growth constraint, not the website quality

Every one of your nineteen pages is a service: flat roofs, fibreglass, chimney repairs, tiled, slate, leadwork, new roofs, felt, rubber, roof repairs, emergency roofers, roof cleaning, fascias and soffits, guttering, dry ridge and dry verge, plus home, contact, privacy and gallery.

Not one of them is a place. The word "Fife" appears 11 times across the homepage. Glenrothes, Kirkcaldy, Dunfermline, St Andrews, Leven, Cupar, Cowdenbeath, Lochgelly, Methil, Buckhaven, Markinch, Burntisland, Dalgety Bay, Inverkeithing and Anstruther appear zero times between them.

That matters because of how people actually search. Almost nobody types "roofing services". They type "roofer Kirkcaldy", "roof repair Dunfermline", "emergency roofer near me". A page titled "Roof Repairs Fife" competes for one of those. A page titled "Roof Repairs in Kirkcaldy" competes for all three.

You told us your radius is 30–40 miles but you prefer the shorter runs. Town pages are how you bias the enquiries toward the shorter runs — you rank hardest where you most want to work, rather than taking whatever Fife sends you.

You already have the hard part. Nineteen service pages is the expensive bit. Crossing them against your best six or eight towns is the cheap bit, and it is the single largest piece of unclaimed ground you have.

Finding 3 — The Form Exists On One Page Out Of Nineteen
Verified, including the part that looked worse than it is

You do have a working enquiry form. It is on /contact-us/, it is a Gravity Form, and it collects name, phone, email, postcode and address. That is a properly built form and it works.

It is the only one. Every other page — including all fifteen service pages — carries three tappable phone numbers and no form at all.

So someone reading your flat roofs page at 9pm, who is not going to ring a tradesman at 9pm, has exactly two options: find the contact page themselves, or leave. Most leave. Your service pages are where the intent is and they have nowhere to put it.

The fix is a short form at the foot of every service page — four fields, not twenty — asking which service, which town, and roughly what the job is. That last question also starts sorting a £100 call-out from a £20,000 re-roof before you drive anywhere.

Finding 4 — Checkatrade Brings You The Work And Your Site Barely Mentions It
The proof you have earned is not on the page

You told us Checkatrade is where your enquiries come from. On your own website, Checkatrade is mentioned once. TrustATrade once. The word "guarantee" once. "Insured" twice. There is no rating, no review count, no number of jobs completed, and no review text anywhere on the homepage.

That is backwards, and it costs you in two directions at once:

  • On Checkatrade you compete on price, next to every other roofer in Fife, on a profile you do not own, for a lead they also sell to others.
  • On your own site you have no proof at all — so the one place you could win on trust instead of price is the one place you do not use it.

Whatever rating and review count you have earned on Checkatrade should be the first thing on your homepage, with real reviews underneath and photographs of real roofs. You already did the work. It is just filed on somebody else's website.

Finding 5 — A Hundred Pounds And Twenty Thousand Come Through The Same Door
Worth deciding before more traffic arrives

You described your job values as running from around £100 up to £10,000–£20,000, and said you are happy to take any size. Fair enough — small jobs keep crews busy and turn into big ones.

But your website makes no distinction anywhere. Roof cleaning and guttering sit in the same list as new roofs, with the same prominence and the same single phone number. So a gutter clear and a full re-roof arrive through the identical route, and you cannot tell which is which until you are on the phone — or worse, in the van.

At 3–4 jobs a week, your constraint is not enquiries, it is which enquiries get your Tuesday. Two or three qualifying questions on the form, and a page that frames the larger work as the main offer, changes the mix without changing the volume.

What Is Genuinely Good — And We Will Say So On The Call
Do not let anyone rebuild this site
  • Your structured data is correct and complete. Organization, LocalBusiness, PostalAddress, WebSite and BreadcrumbList are all present and valid. Most roofers we audit have none of it. Two of the three competitors we looked at for another Suffolk client did not have it either.
  • Nineteen properly separated service pages. This is the expensive, slow part of SEO and it is done.
  • Three tappable phone numbers on every page. For a trade where most enquiries are phone calls, that is right.
  • A real, working enquiry form that captures postcode — which matters when you are deciding whether a job is a short run or a long one.
  • The site loads over HTTPS, is mobile-formatted and has a clean title and meta description.

Nothing in this audit is an argument for a new website. It is an argument for finishing this one.

The Order We Would Do It In
  • 1. Put GA4 in the tag manager that is already there and set conversion tracking on form submits and phone taps. One afternoon. Until this exists, everything else is guesswork.
  • 2. Put your Checkatrade rating, review count and real reviews on the homepage. Free. You have already earned it.
  • 3. Add a four-field form to all fifteen service pages. Which service, which town, rough description, phone.
  • 4. Build town pages for your best six to eight towns — starting with the short runs you actually prefer.
  • 5. Claim and complete the Google Business Profile, with real install photos and a review request after every job.
  • 6. Then, and only then, consider paid traffic. Sending paid clicks into a site with no tracking is paying to learn nothing.

Three or four jobs a week, and no way to tell where any of them came from.

The tracking is an afternoon. The towns are the growth.
